# Creative direction and diversity system

Read when naming, designing, comparing, or art-directing LuvNote templates. Aim for emotional storytelling and visual differentiation rather than novelty for novelty's sake.

## Creative brief (fill only what is relevant)

- **Occasion + relationship:** what the sender wants to say, and to whom.
- **Emotional arc:** initial curiosity → anticipation → meaningful reveal → afterglow.
- **Metaphor:** one coherent world (for example a train ticket, window, music box, paper theater, rainy café, telescope, or keepsake drawer).
- **Signature interaction:** what the receiver physically does and why that action expresses the message.
- **Composition:** distinct layout, typographic contrast, photo placement and screen pacing.
- **Assets:** what can be generated/created versus what requires a reviewed license.
- **Practicalities:** no photo, 1 photo, maximum photos; long Thai text, emojis, mobile portrait, landscape, muted audio, skip/replay.
- **One-sentence pitch:** “The receiver [action], so they discover [emotional payoff].”

## Diversity check

Before approving a concept, compare it to the nearest **existing** theme or prototype, not an abstract inspiration board. In the repo, the Template Lab has used these IDs: `love-letter`, `polaroid`, `cinema`, `stars`, `garden`, `capsule`, `scrapbook`, `world`, `choose`, `diary`, `constellation`, `surprise` (verify current list). These IDs are comparison anchors, not proof of a particular visual implementation.

Compare six axes:

| Axis | Distinctive difference to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Narrative | New story structure, order, agency, or meaning |
| Metaphor | New object/world or expressive language, not a renamed envelope |
| Layout | Different spatial logic: immersive, editorial, side-scrolling, layered, stage, etc. |
| Main interaction | Mechanically and emotionally different gesture or decision |
| Visual identity | Typography, lighting, shape, image treatment, texture and palette as a coherent system |
| Motion rhythm | Different staging and physicality, not the same tween with different easing |

**Decision rule:** If the concept meaningfully changes fewer than three axes, revise the concept. Changing only palette and decoration never qualifies. Do not game the checklist: a change must be obvious when two experiences are viewed side by side. Prefer *one* confident experience over a pile of effects.

## Direction seeds — ideas, not mandatory templates

These are conceptual examples; compare with current code and discard any that duplicate it.

1. **Midnight Radio:** Tune a dial to find a secret message; warm analog grain, frequency typography, intimate sound-free fallback.
2. **Paper Theater:** Drag a scene curtain to reveal layered memories; cut-paper depth, purposeful parallax, theatrical ending.
3. **The Last Train:** Stamp a ticket to visit milestones; destination typography, station lighting, one chapter per stop.
4. **Windows at Dusk:** Open illuminated apartment windows; each reveals a message fragment, soft architectural atmosphere.
5. **Time Capsule Shelf:** Choose and pull out keepsakes; tactile drawer physics, deliberate pacing and a final note.
6. **Rainy Café:** Clear condensation from a glass to uncover a dedication; quiet atmosphere and readable text fallback.
7. **Origami Wishes:** Fold/unfold a paper form that transforms into a message; strong silhouettes, no arbitrary card flip.
8. **One Little Planet:** Rotate a tiny world to discover places meaningful to the pair; lightweight 2D/3D fallback.
9. **A Letter from Tomorrow:** Reveal future wishes by moving through a clock/calendar metaphor; reflective, not gimmicky.
10. **Gallery after Hours:** Walk between carefully staged exhibits of memories; dramatic typography and restrained lighting.

Do not blindly implement all seeds or copy reference imagery. Select ideas that serve the occasion and are meaningfully distinct from shipped templates.

## Common failure modes

- Every concept is a centered greeting card with an “open” button.
- All reveals are the same envelope, spin, confetti, falling hearts or particle shower.
- Effects obscure the message; the recipient does not know what to press.
- Visually impressive hero has no real middle or emotional finish.
- Long Thai messages overflow, gallery images crop key faces, or mobile buttons are too small.
- Preview shows a glamorous state that cannot be reached with actual user-entered content.

## Design handoff format

Describe: creative title; audience; emotion; metaphor; first three seconds; story beats; signature gesture; layout and typographic treatment; interactive state map; mobile and accessibility fallback; optional assets; nearest existing comparison; implementation risk. Use annotated screenshots/previews when available.
